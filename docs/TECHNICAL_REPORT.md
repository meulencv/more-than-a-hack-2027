# CabraLabs: possession labels for FC Barcelona vs Athletic Club

More Than A Hack 2027, Metrica Sports challenge. Technical document of the submitted possession file. 17 November 2026.

## 1. What is submitted

One CSV with a label for each of the 199,319 frames of the tactical video: H 50,302, A 35,275, D 73,280, X 40,462 (the X frames are exactly the ungraded ones). It is regenerated by one command (section 9) from the organisers' data bundle and 3.2 MB of fitted objects: a ball detector, four possession networks and a spell verifier. sha256 of the file: `c1ccf161b837790d50b0b3848cf9ee3a4a37a0b3919c725132dd322fba0aa35e`.

No person labelled or corrected any frame of the challenge match. Every human-made label used for training comes from other matches; on the challenge match the only training signals are pseudo-labels produced by algorithms (section 5).

## 2. Pipeline

1. **Tracking and camera alignment.** Both automatic tracking files are parsed. The broadcast timeline is mapped to the tactical one automatically by matching the constellation of each team's players (the ball is not used, no offset was set by hand): 80,443 frames aligned in 84 runs; where both cameras see the ball, the median distance between the two is 0.75 m.
2. **Ball from the tactical video.** A small detector, trained on this video with the tracking's own ball as the only label, and a tracker fill the gaps of the tracking's ball. Graded frames with a ball go from 59.3% to 74.4%; the broadcast ball then adds 1,567 frames (75.4%).
3. **Frozen ensemble.** Four networks with equal weight, trained on six DFL/Sportec open matches: two temporal CNNs on hand-built features (123 features; the second adds 33 physical-control features) and two per-player trajectory networks (two seeds). They output P(H), P(A), P(D) at 5 Hz, plus a boundary score.
4. **Camera-operator fusion** (section 3.4) and the published second-half kick-off.
5. **Decoder.** Probabilities are interpolated to 25 Hz and decoded per half by a three-state Viterbi with switching cost 4 between teams and 2.5 to or from dead ball, reduced where the boundary score is high. No minimum duration is imposed.
6. **Spell verifier** (section 3.2). Each decoded possession of 8 s or less is a candidate for removal. On the challenge match 65 are removed: 373 possessions become 290, and 2.1% of graded frames change.
7. **Kick-off law.** The second half is started by the team that did not take the first-half kick-off (Law 8). The decode gives the first 1.3 s of the second half to Athletic, who also kicked off the first half, and then switches to Barcelona. The step checks the law against the tracking (the team of the player nearest to the centre spot in the second before each published kick-off: Athletic at 0.5 m in the first half, Barcelona at 2.1 m in the second) and, because both agree and that first possession is short (3 s or less) and immediately followed by Barcelona, gives those frames to Barcelona. It changes 32 frames (31 from A to H; the one graded frame before the published kick-off becomes D). It has no fitted parameter; its two constants are fixed (only a first possession of at most 3 s is reassigned; the centre-spot check uses the last second before the kick-off), and the two frames it refers to are the published kick-offs. The rule was prompted by the decode contradicting the law and is decided by the tracking and the decode alone (`possession/v31_law.py`). 290 possessions become 289.
8. **CSV.** Graded frames get the decoded label, all other frames get X.

Steps 1 to 5 without step 2 are the system of the earlier rounds (history in `docs/`, in Spanish). Steps 2, 6 and 7 are what this document adds.

## 3. Why this design

### 3.1 The finding: the networks learned a different "possession"

The networks were trained on the DFL/Sportec open matches, whose native per-frame possession label switches much more often than the challenge's (in practice at almost every touch; this is our reading of the statistics below, not the dataset's stated definition). The challenge asks for the provider's *controlled* possession. The two differ by about a factor of two in how many possessions a match has:

| Source | Possessions per live minute | Mean length (s) | Shorter than 3 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| DFL native labels, six training matches | 7.1–11.0 | 5.5–8.4 | 39–50% |
| Metrica open games, event reference, "consistent" reading | 4.1–4.35 | 13.8–14.7 | 18–22% |
| Metrica open games, event reference, "alternative" reading | 5.8–6.2 | 9.7–10.4 | 33–36% |
| Challenge public window, published summary (`DATA.md`) | 4.2 | 14.2 | not published |
| Frozen ensemble decoded on the Metrica open games | 5.1–5.9 | 10.1–11.7 | 24–29% |
| Challenge match, pipeline before the verifier | 6.4 | 9.4 | 32% |
| Challenge match, submitted file | 5.1 | 11.8 | 19% |

The rows of the external games and of the earlier candidates are in `docs/evidence/measure.json`. The two readings are two conversions of Metrica's own event annotations of its three open sample games into H/A/D (`possession/reference.py`, `possession/audit_reference.py`). In the *consistent* reading an interception or clearance that the provider logs as an immediate loss of the ball (a one-touch interception) does not change possession, which is the written rule of `CHALLENGE.md` (a touch without control, a deflection, an aerial duel without control). In the *alternative* reading every such touch starts a possession, except a recovery lost again within 0.2 s. Neither is a manual labelling; both are conversions written by us. For game 3, whose event file has another format (a carry after every recovery), the consistent reference drops the carry events so that the same touch rule applies as in games 1 and 2; with the converter as first written, game 3 has 378 possessions instead of 282 and behaves like the alternative reading.

On the Metrica games the decoded ensemble sits between the two readings (row 5). On the challenge match it is more fragmented than either: 6.5 possessions per live minute before the video ball, 6.4 with it. spellF1 scores possessions as objects, so one spurious two-second flip inside a long possession replaces one predicted possession by three, of which at most one can match the true one.

### 3.2 Spell verifier: a correction at the level the metric scores

Retraining the networks on other labels was tried and is not submitted (section 4). The submitted correction works on the decoded output. A candidate is a predicted possession of at most 8 s with one of these removals: between two possessions of the other team it is absorbed (three possessions become one); next to a stoppage it becomes dead ball, or goes to the neighbouring team. A gradient-boosting regressor (scikit-learn, 150 trees of at most 6 leaves) predicts the change in spellF1 that the removal causes, from 23 features of the ensemble output only: duration and context, probabilities inside and around the possession, boundary scores, decoder margin. It is fitted on the 2,483 candidate removals the same ensemble produces on the three Metrica open games (each game under three sensor conditions: the published tracking and two simulated degradations), where the exact change is computed with the official metric. Removals are applied best first, in up to three passes.

**Uncertain semantics.** The private reference is derived from corrected tracking, and nobody outside the organisers can know how it treats one-touch interceptions. So the training target is the *expected* gain under a prior over the two readings, and the prior is the equal one:

`target = 0.5 x gain under consistent + 0.5 x gain under alternative`, remove if the prediction is above 0.

0.5 is the indifference prior: it takes no side between the two readings of the written definition and uses no information about the challenge match. The boundary 0 was not tuned. An earlier default of this pipeline put 0.7 on the consistent reading; that number was informed by the public window and was replaced for that reason (section 6). The weights 0.7 and 1.0 appear in section 3.3 as alternatives that are not submitted.

They pay for it under the alternative reading, where they lose 0.014 to 0.042 and 0.047 to 0.083 (worst single game and condition: −0.056 and −0.103). **Frozen base or blend.** The blend rows have the largest gains under the consistent reading, but under the alternative one they lose 0.047 to 0.090 where the submitted file loses nothing, their relabelling rule was calibrated on these same Metrica games, and their networks were never checked on a video-filled ball. The simpler pipeline with the smaller loss was chosen. That choice was made after seeing this table, on the same three games.

### 3.4 Ball from the video, and the camera operators

**Detector.** The tracking has no ball on 40.7% of graded frames. A 0.7 MB fully-convolutional network looks at three frames (t−2, t, t+2) of the 1080p tactical video. Its only training signal is the tracking's own ball projected into the image with the provided homography and re-centred on the visible blob; three rounds of self-training add hard negatives. One-minute blocks, every eighth minute, never enter training. Detections are mapped to the pitch, linked into tracklets, and a dynamic programme keeps the track that is physically possible and agrees with the tracking's ball at both ends of each gap. It fills 23,996 of the 64,643 graded gap frames.

**Held-out accuracy** (`docs/evidence/video_ball_f2.json`; 12,808 held-out frames where the tracking has a ball, which is hidden from the tracker): a ball is reported on 92.1% of them; of those, 63.8% lie within 1.5 m and 78.3% within 3 m of the tracking's ball, median error 0.93 m; 12.4% are more than 5 m away. The detection threshold was chosen on these same held-out minutes, so the figures are slightly optimistic.

**Airborne balls.** Of that 12.4%, 10.5 points are detections straight above the tracked ground point in the image: a ball in the air, which the ground-plane homography maps to a point farther from the camera. Only 1.8 points are false tracks. A flight filter that removes such stretches lowers the far share to 4.9% but reports a ball on only 67.7% of frames. It is switched off because of the test below.

**Hide-and-fill ablation** (`python -m possession.v26_ablation`, which writes `data/work/v26/ablation.json`; copy in `docs/evidence/ablation.json`). In the held-out minutes 40% of the tracked ball is hidden in simulated gaps with the lengths of real gaps (three seeds), and the frozen ensemble is run again. The reference is the ensemble's own output with the real ball, not a ground truth.

| Hidden frames given to the networks as | Same label as with the real ball | Mean absolute change of P(H) − P(A) | Mean absolute change of P(D) | spellF1 against the real-ball output, mean (per seed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no ball | 86.1% | 0.234 | 0.130 | 0.889 (0.915 / 0.874 / 0.877) |
| video ball, flight filter on | 90.7% | 0.123 | 0.050 | 0.895 (0.884 / 0.892 / 0.909) |
| video ball, flight filter off (submitted) | 91.9% | 0.107 | 0.033 | 0.908 (0.876 / 0.916 / 0.931) |

What it shows: with the video ball the probabilities move back toward what the networks say with the real ball, and more so without the filter. What it does not show: an effect on spellF1 that can be relied on (positive on average, negative on one seed of three, 60 possessions per seed), or anything about real gaps, 85% of which lie where the candidate without the video ball already says dead ball. On the challenge match the video ball changes 2.0% of graded frames.

**Camera-operator fusion.** Stoppages are where the tracking is least informative. The two recordings hold independent evidence: the TV director leaves the main camera mostly during stoppages, and both cameras pan and zoom differently then. A gradient-boosting student with 19 camera-only features (cut state and timing, pan, zoom, focus point of both cameras; no player, no ball) learns dead/alive from the ensemble's own decisions, cross-fitted over contiguous blocks so it cannot copy the decision it is scored on (AUC against the ensemble 0.91). Its output is combined with the ensemble's P(D) as independent evidence in logit space, clipped to [0.02, 0.98]. Graded frames before the published second-half kick-off (frame 113,742, `FAQ.md`) are set to dead ball.

## 4. Assets used, and what was tried and dropped

**Used:** tactical tracking (players and ball); tactical video (ball detector); tactical homography (pitch-to-image projection, camera focus and zoom); broadcast tracking (second ball, constellations for the alignment); broadcast homography (cut detection and camera motion). The broadcast video is not used.

**Tried and dropped:**

- **Smart Tagging.** All 638 tag instances were characterised without labels: lead and lag of each code, where the core of a phase sits, how well a tag predicts team and dead/alive on the ensemble's confident frames. Fused as a bounded evidence term it changed 4.3% of graded frames. Rejected by a label-free check: on the frames where it flipped the team and a ball was close, the nearest player belonged to the new team in 20% of cases, against 86% agreement between nearest player and label on unchanged frames.
- **Referee whistle (audio).** 40 whistles found from a template of the two kick-off whistles; 14 fall inside play according to the ensemble. Left off (see section 6).
- **Segment-level and minimum-Bayes-risk decoding.** Duration-aware and calibrated segment decoders and their consensus: +0.066 to +0.073 under the consistent reading (+0.070 for minimum-Bayes-risk), against +0.074 for a verifier with the consistent target; stacked on the verifier, up to +0.079. Parity within the noise, with more parameters; none beat the verifier on every game.
- **Physical per-possession features** (ball-to-player distances, carries, ball speed) in the verifier. They help on clean tracking (+0.024) and not under the stress condition (+0.001), and they are the features that most separate the challenge match from the Metrica games (domain AUC 0.94, against 0.60–0.70 between Metrica games). In the study of section 3.3 the verifier with these features and the 0.5 target has the same value at the equal prior as the submitted one (+0.029 for both); it is not submitted because of this shift.
- **Retraining the networks on controlled possession.** DFL labels converted by a tracking rule (a possession between two of the other team counts only if the ball stays within 1.5 m of that team for 1.2 s). Larger gain under the consistent reading, larger loss under the alternative one (section 3.3). Kept as a flag of the reproduction command, not submitted.
- **Where the remaining error is** (error budget on the open games, `possession/v30b_budget.py`). With the predicted stoppages and the reference's team layer the score would be 0.871 / 0.896 (consistent / alternative; the submitted recipe has 0.702 / 0.699); with every boundary within 2 s moved onto the reference it would be 0.736 / 0.734. The loss is in which short possessions exist, not in timing. A learned boundary re-timer gains +0.007 / +0.008 from the network outputs (+0.013 / +0.013 with ball kinematics added), inside the noise; its onset correction is calibrated on a lag that is smaller on the challenge match (label-free check: median delay from the ball crossing a line to the decoded stoppage about 0.3 s there, about 0.7 s on the open games). Not adopted.
- **Sound and image of both recordings** (`possession/v30e_*.py`). A two-tone referee-whistle detector that requires both recordings (63 whistles), the burnt-in scoreboard (three score changes, all inside decoded stoppages), frame-difference and grass-share statistics. As per-frame dead/alive evidence they add nothing to the camera student (AUC against the ensemble 0.910 with the camera features, 0.912 with the sound and image features added). Stoppage onsets moved to a whistle or to the ball crossing a line: the part that can be scored on the open games gains +0.005, and the whistle part cannot be scored anywhere. Not adopted.
- **Networks trained on the provider's own open games** (`possession/v30d_*.py`, `v31c_*.py`; leave-one-game-out, two seeds). With the consistent target: 0.764 under the consistent reading and 0.628 under the alternative one (submitted recipe 0.702 / 0.699), so the equal-prior value is lower (0.696 against 0.700). A review found that these networks had learned an export property of the open games: their ball is absent exactly while play is dead (88–92% of stoppage starts lie within 0.4 s of the ball vanishing). Retrained on views whose ball availability carries no label timing, the in-domain networks are level with the frozen ones (alternative target, with the equal-prior verifier: 0.701 / 0.696 against 0.702 / 0.699; decoder only 0.664 / 0.694 against 0.651 / 0.692; on the review's label-blind test conditions +0.019 / +0.018, inside the noise). Not adopted.
- **A compromise target.** One labelling of the open games scores at least 0.85 under both readings: the alternative reference with possessions shorter than 2 s between two possessions of the other team absorbed. A verifier, a second-stage re-reading of the network outputs, a DFL-only retraining and an in-domain retraining were each aimed at it (`possession/v31a_*.py`, `v31b_*.py`, `v31c_*.py`). All of them gain under the consistent reading and lose under the alternative one, on every game: for example the second stage +0.037 / −0.030 and the in-domain networks +0.019 / −0.034 against the submitted recipe; the DFL-only networks reach an equal-prior value of 0.697 (0.699 blended 50/50 with the frozen ones) against 0.700. The highest equal-prior value in this family is 0.710 (the second stage blended 50/50 with the frozen outputs, +0.034 / −0.015), inside the noise and still a loss under the alternative reading on every game. Averaging the networks over a mirrored pitch changes −0.002 / −0.001 (the team-swapped view was already averaged). Not adopted. Each of these negative results was re-run by a separate internal review (its scripts are not in the repository, and parts of the study code of these four items read that review's files, so they cannot be rerun from the published files alone).
- **Earlier model families** (documented in `docs/`): gradient boosting on per-frame features, single temporal CNNs, auxiliary reconstruction, full attention and Bi-LSTM variants, broadcast-domain adaptation with SkillCorner open data, 41 alternatives on duration, transitions and touch confirmation. Each was superseded or failed the adoption rule written before it was run.

## 6. Use of the public 5-minute window

**Scored.** The organisers' scorer was run on candidate files on the public window at several points of the project, as a check that the pipeline is correct (frame alignment, label meaning, file format). It scores every file in the results folder and prints them ranked, so each run also showed how the candidates of that moment compared. One of those runs, on 16 November, covered all candidates that existed then: 31 files, the four organiser baselines included. Its rows for the stages of the submitted pipeline and for four of the five alternatives of the reproduction command (the fifth, `final`, is the submitted file without the kick-off law; it did not exist then and has the same labels as the submitted file on the window):

| File | spellF1 | macroF1 | Possessions (reference: 17) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen ensemble (earlier rounds) | 0.626 | 0.841 | 22 |
| + camera fusion | 0.653 | 0.902 | 21 |
| + video ball (the pipeline before the verifier) | 0.709 | 0.912 | 18 |
| Not submitted: + verifier, 0.7/0.3 (the default at that time) | 0.667 | 0.918 | 16 |
| Not submitted: + verifier, minimum of both gains | 0.709 | 0.912 | 18 |
| Not submitted: blend + verifier, 0.7/0.3 | 0.676 | 0.902 | 12 |
| Not submitted: blend + verifier, consistent only | 0.622 | 0.897 | 10 |

The submitted file (verifier with the equal prior) did not exist at that time. It was scored on the window once, alone, on 17 November, after it had been fixed and after every decision of sections 3 and 4 had been taken, as a last check of the pipeline: spellF1 0.709, macroF1 0.912, 18 possessions. These are the values of the pipeline before the verifier, because on these five minutes the equal-prior verifier removes no possession. Nothing was changed after that run. On this window the 0.7 verifier lowers spellF1 (0.709 to 0.667) while it raises macroF1. With 17 possessions one possession moves spellF1 by about six points (`EVALUATION.md`), so we draw no conclusion from this table in either direction. Earlier in the project, a candidate that scored 0.707 was replaced by later ones that scored 0.659 and 0.626.

**Inspected.** The label runs of the window were compared with the predictions of the candidate of that time, to understand what the reference considers a possession (as `CHALLENGE.md` invites) and where the candidate failed. Three things were seen: stoppages that the candidate started late, short possession flips that the reference does not have, and two very short possessions in the reference (0.76 s and 1.32 s). That inspection motivated the camera features (the director's cuts coincide with stoppages), the decision to leave the whistle component off (one whistle during live play split a correct possession there), and the idea of a verifier that works on whole possessions. Smart Tagging was first set aside after looking at its phases on the window; it was tested again later without labels (section 4).

**Case study.** On the evening of 16 November, after the equal-prior file had been fixed, the window was looked at once more with a tool that logs every call (`possession/v30a_cases.py`): its 20 label transitions, most of them frame by frame, and the stretches where an opponent is tracked within 2.5 m of the ball without the label changing, at one frame in four or five, were viewed on contact sheets of the tactical video, next to the tracking and to the whistle blasts of the tactical audio, to describe in words how the reference labels cases (as `CHALLENGE.md` invites). The same four questions (when a restart goes live, where a stoppage starts, where a switch is placed, how common very short possessions are) were computed for each external reference (`possession/v30a_external.py`), with the aim of choosing the closest construction if the window pointed to one. Nothing was fitted or scored on the window, no label was produced and no constant was taken from it. What it found: switches are placed at the gaining team's touch or up to about half a second later, never at the losing team's last touch; restarts go live shortly after the kick; foul stoppages start after the whistle rather than at the contact; and two of the 17 possessions come from a single uncontrolled touch in a duel, which is an event of the alternative reading, while the number and length of possessions are those of the consistent reading. It points both ways on the reading, so no construction was chosen and the prior stayed at 0.5. The stoppage re-timers of section 4, which move onsets earlier, were evaluated the same evening, during and after the case study; they were rejected on the open games (gain inside the noise) and on a label-free comparison with the challenge match, and the case study, known by then, points the same way. The log of what was viewed describes the match frame by frame and is therefore kept outside the repository. None of the candidates of section 4 that came after it (error budget, sound and image, in-domain networks, compromise target) was scored on the window. The kick-off law of section 2 was written after the case study, which had looked at the first-half kick-off. It changes only frames of the second half, which the window does not cover; its one input from the first half, which team kicked off, is taken from the decode and the tracking (the player on the centre spot), and it reads no label.

**What does not depend on the window.** No model, threshold, weight or prior of the submitted pipeline is fitted on the window or derived from it, and no label of it is used for training. The verifier is fitted only on the three Metrica open games, and its prior between the two readings is 0.5. The full-match baseline scores published in `EVALUATION.md` were not used to infer anything about the private reference. What traces back to the window is the list of design decisions in the paragraph **Inspected** above, that is, which components exist.

**History of the prior.** Until the final audit of 16 November the default of this pipeline was the same verifier with 0.7 on the consistent reading. That 0.7 was a judgement informed by the window: five-minute windows of the open games, under each reading, were compared with its published summary (`DATA.md`, `README.md`: 17 possessions of mean 14.2 s in 241 s of live play), with the number of possessions our own prediction has on the same frames (21), and with the lengths of its two shortest possessions, which are not published and were known from the inspection. `RULES.md` says the public window is not a tuning set. The audit flagged the 0.7 as the one constant of the pipeline that depended on the window, and the submission was changed to the equal prior for that reason. The earlier file stays reproducible (`--variant prior70`). When the change was made, the scores in the table above were known, and so was the 0.5 row of section 3.3. The submitted file had not been scored when the change was made, and the three files that scored highest in that run (0.709: the pipeline before the verifier, the same pipeline with an earlier version of the video ball, and the minimum-target verifier) are not submitted. One fact points the other way and is stated for completeness: the equal prior removes fewer possessions than the 0.7 prior (65 against 88), which moves the file toward the pipeline without the verifier, the direction in which the scored files do better on this window. The later run confirmed it: on the window the submitted file has the same labels as the pipeline before the verifier and ties with those three files at 0.709 (paragraph **Scored**).

## 7. Possession rules implemented

- **Loose and contested balls.** Possession stays with the last team. This is not a nearest-player rule: the networks use temporal context and the decoder charges a cost for every change, so a change needs sustained evidence for the other team. A missing ball is neither a stoppage nor a change.
- **Touches, deflections, aerial duels, one-touch interceptions.** A possession of 8 s or less between two possessions of the other team is absorbed when the verifier predicts a positive expected gain: 18 of 104 such possessions on the challenge match. There is no fixed duration threshold; a short possession with high and sustained probability for its team is kept. After the verifier no possession is shorter than 1 s (the shortest lasts 1.12 s).
- **Short possessions next to a stoppage.** The verifier removes 47 of them: 44 become dead ball and 3 go to the previous team. We read most of them as the ball being handled before a restart or play carried on after the whistle, which the reference marks as dead ball.
- **Dead ball and restarts.** D comes from the ensemble's P(D) combined with the camera evidence. Play restarts when that evidence turns live; the team after a restart is whatever the networks see, with no carry-over from before the stoppage and no explicit set-piece detector. H D H is two possessions.
- **Second-half kick-off.** The 2,934 graded frames before the published kick-off are D. The possession that starts at the kick-off belongs to the team that did not kick off the first half (step 7 of section 2).
- **No abstention on graded frames.** The scorer fills an X with the previous label before matching possessions, so on the ranking metric an X is a repeated label, and under macroF1 it is a miss. The decoder's answer is always written; X appears only on the 40,462 ungraded frames.

## 8. Expectations and limitations

- **The external estimate is not the challenge score.** On the Metrica open games the verifier moves spellF1 from about 0.65 to about 0.70 under the consistent reading and from 0.69 to 0.70 under the alternative one, with references built from events and three games that were reused during development. On the public window the submitted file scores 0.709, the same as the pipeline without the verifier (it was scored once, after it had been fixed; section 6); the 0.7 verifier scores 0.667 there. The score on the full match is unknown.
- **The challenge is harder than the validation.** Its tracking has no ball on 41% of graded frames, and the verifier is applied to probabilities that went through the video ball and the camera fusion, neither of which exists for the external games. The verifier's inputs on the challenge match are distinguishable from those it was fitted on (domain AUC 0.88, against 0.72–0.73 between Metrica games).
- **Semantics.** The equal prior is a hedge. If the private reference does not count one-touch interceptions, the submitted file gains about 0.02 less than the 0.7 variant did on the open games (+0.051 against +0.073 on average); if it counts them all, the verifier is expected to change the score very little (section 3.3).
- **Dead-ball onset and restart timing** remain the largest error source that could not be validated. The networks learned stoppages on tracking where they are almost error-free; the camera student inherits the ensemble's systematic biases; quick restarts shown on the main camera depend on the tracking alone.
- **Airborne balls.** With the flight filter off, about one filled ball in ten is displaced by the ground-plane projection.
- **Reproducibility.** A bit-identical detector pass was verified on one Apple Silicon machine (MPS, float16). On CPU it takes about a day and is not guaranteed to give the same file: on a 300-frame sample 2 of 973 detections fell on the other side of the confidence threshold.
- **With more time:** a restart detector and dead-ball validation on another automatically tracked match; a ball-height estimate instead of the ground-plane assumption; a verifier fitted on outputs that include the video ball and camera fusion; more external games labelled with controlled possession; a deterministic CPU detector pass.


Python 3.12.15, torch 2.15.0, numpy 2.5.4, scikit-learn 1.9.1, OpenCV 5.0.0; reference machine Apple M5 Max, macOS 27.0.1.
